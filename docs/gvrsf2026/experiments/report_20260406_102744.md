## Abstract

This bundle tests whether a decentralized recurrent MAPPO swarm still shows the same main scientific patterns after a fresh full rerun and whether those patterns now remain visible up to `30` agents. The broad campaign re-tested curriculum quality, baseline superiority, robustness, generic pheromone ablation, and general swarm-size scaling. The focused killer campaign re-tested the central stigmergy claim using a repeated-source route-reuse task and extended the primary upper-bound matched test to `30` agents.

The broad campaign again showed that the current curriculum-trained policy outperformed the weaker older training configuration (`1.25` vs `0.00` food delivered per episode, Welch's `p = 0.000828`), beat both rule-based and random baselines (`1.25` vs `0.30` and `0.05`), and scaled strongly with swarm size (`0.05` deliveries at `1` agent vs `4.65` at `30` agents). The generic broad pheromone ablation remained weak (`1.25` vs `1.05`, `p = 0.625`), which again justifies the need for a stronger mechanism-specific experiment.

The killer experiment provided that stronger test. At `6` agents, the pheromone-trained swarm again outperformed the no-pheromone-trained control (`1.32` vs `0.00`, paired `p = 0.00653`). At the new `30`-agent upper bound, the pheromone-trained swarm achieved `2.66` mean deliveries versus `0.00` for the no-pheromone-trained control, with paired t-test `p = 0.000847` and Wilcoxon `p = 9.96e-06`. Late deliveries were also significantly higher (`1.12` vs `0.00`, paired `p = 0.01868`).

Together, these results support the same core conclusion as before, but now with a fresh full rerun and an extended scaling range: collective intelligence in this system emerges from decentralized interaction, and stigmergic communication is a key mechanism that helps that intelligence scale.

## 1. Background

This project studies a decentralized swarm foraging problem. Agents must explore an environment, discover food, pick it up, return to a nest, and deliver it. Each agent acts from local observation only. There is no global planner during evaluation.

The main scientific idea is **stigmergy**: indirect coordination through changes to the environment. In biological swarms this is often implemented by pheromone trails. In this project, the same idea is tested computationally by allowing agents to deposit and later benefit from pheromone-like environmental traces.

The full foraging loop is:

`explore -> discover -> pick up -> return -> deliver`

This matters because many weaker policies can partially solve the task by wandering into targets, but fail to convert discoveries into reliable deliveries.

## 2. Research Questions

### 2.1 Broad algorithmic question

Does the current curriculum-trained decentralized MAPPO system still outperform weaker training and simpler baselines, and does it remain meaningfully effective under harder conditions and larger swarm sizes?

### 2.2 Core scientific question

Does pheromone-mediated stigmergy provide a measurable coordination advantage that remains visible at larger swarm sizes, including `30` agents?

## 3. Hypotheses

### 3.1 Main hypothesis

If collective intelligence is emerging through decentralized stigmergic coordination, then pheromone-trained swarms should significantly outperform no-pheromone-trained swarms in repeated-source foraging, especially in late-episode and post-discovery behavior and especially at larger swarm sizes.

### 3.2 Supporting hypotheses

- the current curriculum should outperform the weaker older training configuration
- the learned policy should outperform rule-based and random baselines
- aggregate task completion should increase with swarm size
- performance should degrade under harder conditions, but not collapse completely in every case

## 8. Results

## 8.1 Experiment Family 1: Curriculum Learning vs Weaker Training

### Test question

Does the stronger current curriculum produce a measurably better final policy than an older weaker training setup?

### Results

From [tables/family_summary.csv](./20260406_102744/tables/family_summary.csv):

- current curriculum checkpoint:
  - mean `food_delivered = 1.25`
  - mean `delivery_conversion = 0.583`
  - mean `exploration_coverage = 0.212`
- weaker older checkpoint:
  - mean `food_delivered = 0.00`
  - mean `delivery_conversion = 0.000`
  - mean `exploration_coverage = 0.146`

From [tables/statistical_tests.csv](./20260406_102744/tables/statistical_tests.csv):

- Welch's t-test on `food_delivered`: `p = 0.000828`
- Cohen's `d = 1.254`

Figure:

### Interpretation

This again shows that the curriculum was a real algorithmic contribution. The weaker model still found and picked up food, but it failed to complete the full delivery loop reliably.

## 10. Limitations

Main limitations:

- the broad rerun needed streamed raw writes plus a post-hoc finalizer because the copied broad runner exited silently after completing all episodes
- the strongest stigmergy claim still comes from the repeated-source route-reuse design, which is narrower than every possible swarm task
- the broad generic pheromone ablation remains weak, showing that not every environment exposes the mechanism equally well
- this bundle is still simulation evidence, not a physical multi-robot validation study

These limitations narrow the claim, but they do not undo the main result.

## 11. Conclusion

This fresh full rerun supports the same central conclusion as the earlier bundle, but now with fresh integrated evidence and stronger scaling coverage:

> In this system, collective intelligence emerges from decentralized interaction, and stigmergic communication is a key mechanism that helps that intelligence scale.

In plain language:

- the curriculum still mattered,
- the learned policy still beat simple baselines,
- the swarm still scaled broadly through `30` agents,
- and the strongest targeted stigmergy test still showed a significant coordination advantage, now confirmed again at `30` agents.
